\begin{abstract}
Linear-cost recurrent denoisers can make repeated long-context diffusion
passes affordable, but extra calls help only when their learned conditionals
use the available evidence. We formulate a cost--error criterion separating
discarded dependence from conditional prediction error, and test its prediction
with a 455M-parameter bidirectional RWKV denoiser on exactly enumerable binary
posterior tasks. From one selected starting checkpoint, three matched pairs
of 600-update adaptations hold training length at 16K and vary public-block
position. Distance-balanced training reduces far-context conditional error
relative to near-only training in every adaptation seed: the mean paired
reduction is 6.051 nats, with a conditional problem-bootstrap 95\% interval
$[5.392,6.735]$. Evidence flips elicit mostly selective responses; an exhaustive
history audit exposes rare collateral errors. A fresh 32-problem layout test
retains the training-arm advantage with nearby instructions and distant task
information, while exposing sensitivity to distractor order. The balanced models'
equal-call contrast agrees with the predicted $4\log2$
dependence reduction to within $1.3\times10^{-4}$ nats. All three balanced
models satisfy a matched empirical quality--cost certificate: recurrent
two-call execution takes 1.30 seconds versus 2.00 for softmax attention under
a predeclared 1.5-second mean budget. All 1008 main-study accuracy conditions are retained.
The result supports a controlled evidence-use mechanism and a restricted
operating point, rather than broad long-context language-model superiority.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Long-context generation must both extract distant evidence and coordinate the
answer tokens that depend on it. Linear attention and recurrent sequence mixers
reduce the arithmetic required to process a long input. Masked diffusion offers
a separate mechanism: successive calls condition unrevealed answer positions on
tokens revealed earlier. Our hypothesis is that combining these mechanisms can
produce a useful accuracy--cost operating point: cheaper full-context scans may
make informative additional denoising calls affordable.

The two mechanisms require separate evidence. Linear sequence scaling does not
guarantee low request latency, and additional calls do not guarantee better
predictions. In particular, a denoiser that ignores previously revealed answer
tokens cannot recover their dependencies merely by executing more calls. Nor
does rereading a long prompt prove that its distant evidence was used. We study
these questions with one recurrent model, one controlled task generator, and a
same-checkpoint intervention on the reveal schedule, together with a matched
training-position intervention at a fixed 16K context length.

The contribution is an experimentally testable cost--error criterion
and its evaluation with learned recurrent conditionals. We use established
conditional-divergence accounting rather than claiming a new KL chain rule.
Likewise, recurrent and linear-attention diffusion already have precedents.
A combined advantage requires both learned long-context competence and a
measured cost advantage. The preceding 1K adaptation fails the 16K
conditional-error requirement. The matched 16K study then holds token budget,
training examples, and inference dependence fixed: covering distant public
queries reduces the measured error across all three adaptation seeds. With
small errors for both reveal policies, their equal-call contrast closely
matches the predicted dependence reduction. Broad checkpoint benchmarks and earlier posterior adaptation
studies are supporting evidence, summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:prior}.

\section{Setting and implementation}
Let $c$ contain $H$ clamped context/query tokens and let $Y\in\{0,1\}^N$ be the
answer. The context is retained in full. A denoising call reads the context and
the current answer canvas, then returns a binary marginal at each answer
position. Previously revealed positions are preserved. For a fixed ordered
partition $\pi=(B_1,\ldots,B_K)$, positions in $B_k$ are independently sampled
using the model predictions conditioned on earlier reveals
$Y_{V_k}$, where $V_k=\bigcup_{j<k}B_j$. The partition is chosen independently
of the sampled values. This is the endpoint law audited below; confidence-ranked
argmax decoding is a different law.

\paragraph{Recurrent denoiser.}
The main model adapts the released approximately 0.4B RWKV-7 backbone
\citep{rwkv7} into a tied bidirectional denoiser with 455,010,304 trainable
parameters. Each call rebuilds transient recurrent states from the current
canvas. It does not carry a scan's terminal state into the next diffusion call.
RWKV-7 is a generalized delta-rule recurrent mixer with linear sequence-length
arithmetic at fixed width; it is not an exact implementation of softmax
attention. We retain the distinction between recurrence implementing a chosen
finite-feature kernel \citep{lineartransformer} and replacing that kernel by a
learned generalized-delta operator.

\paragraph{Training.}
The short-task curriculum first trains two-bit absorbing denoising for 300
updates, then a mixture of ordinary denoising and information-set histories
at four bits through update 700 and eight bits through update 1,500.
A further 1,000 updates supervise paired information-set histories.
Exact conditional soft targets remove avoidable label noise. The policy loss
supervises only the positions revealed at the selected round; future values
are not inputs. The absorbing construction follows masked discrete diffusion
\citep{d3pm,mdlm}. Three data/corruption seeds and both independent and
complementary history branches are retained. The long-context study selects
a competent terminal checkpoint and evaluates a fixed 200-update context
adaptation. The matched 16K follow-up uses six fixed 600-update adaptations
from the selected original checkpoint, as specified below. These are controlled
task adaptations, not language-model pretraining.

\section{A cost--error criterion}
Fix $c$ and write $P=P_c$. For each reveal group define
\begin{align}
 D_\pi(c)&=\sum_k\E_{Y_{V_k}\sim P}
 \KL\!\left(P(Y_{B_k}\mid Y_{V_k},c)\,\middle\Vert\,
       \prod_{i\in B_k}P(Y_i\mid Y_{V_k},c)\right),\\
 E_{M,\pi}(c)&=\sum_k\sum_{i\in B_k}\E_{Y_{V_k}\sim P}
 \KL\!\left(P(Y_i\mid Y_{V_k},c)\,\middle\Vert\,
       q_M^i(Y_i\mid Y_{V_k},c)\right).
\end{align}
The first term is dependence discarded by independent within-group sampling;
the second is error in the learned conditional marginals. With positive model
probability on the target support, the chain rule gives
\begin{equation}
 \KL(P_c\Vert Q_{M,\pi,c})=D_\pi(c)+E_{M,\pi}(c).
 \label{eq:decomposition}
\end{equation}
Conditional total correlation and factorization error are established tools
for analyzing parallel discrete sampling
\citep{factorization2025,ctcdepth2026,intrinsicdependence2026}.
Our use of Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomposition} is diagnostic and predictive: it identifies
the learned quantity that an affordable extra pass must improve.

\begin{proposition}[Same-checkpoint intervention]
For two fixed reveal policies, multiple calls improve forward KL over one call
if and only if
\begin{equation}
 E_{M,\mathrm{multi}}(c)-E_{M,\mathrm{one}}(c)
 <D_{\mathrm{one}}(c)-D_{\mathrm{multi}}(c).
 \label{eq:intervention}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subtract the two instances of Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomposition}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Supervision must cover the inference histories.}
The estimation term also depends on which histories receive supervision.
Let $\mu_\pi$ select a target-distributed policy history and one of the $N$
coordinate predictions uniformly, and let $\nu$ be normalized training
supervision on the same context distribution. If
$d\mu_\pi/d\nu\le\kappa$, then
$E_{M,\pi}\le N\kappa\,\mathcal R_\nu$, where $\mathcal R_\nu$ is excess
conditional cross-entropy above the oracle entropy floor. This standard
change-of-measure bound does not hold with a finite uniform constant for
uncovered histories. Appendix~\ref{app:coverage} gives a counterexample in
which two-call excess error is zero while eight-call error is arbitrarily large.
It also explains why raw minibatch loss is not a held-out competence certificate.
For the implemented eight-stage absorbing objective, the exact worst-case
coverage ratio is $1024$ for either the information-set or sequential policy.
Adding equal-weight information-set supervision reduces its own ratio below
$2$, while the sequential ratio remains about $1988$.
These are population risk-conversion constants, not sample-complexity lower
bounds or guarantees that optimization attains a small risk.


Let $C_M(\pi,c)$ denote measured full-request cost in a specified unit. At fixed
layer count and widths, recurrent mixing has $O(K(H+N))$ arithmetic, while
dense softmax mixing has $O(K(H+N)^2)$ arithmetic. Projections, vocabulary heads,
scan directions, precision, and hardware constants remain part of request
cost. FlashAttention reduces memory traffic without making dense attention
arithmetic linear \citep{flashattention}. The asymptotic distinction motivates
the experiment; it does not supply a measured speedup.

\begin{proposition}[Budget certificate for a declared policy set]
Suppose a budget $B$ permits recurrent model $R$'s multiple-call policy and
attention model $A$'s one-call policy, but excludes all other policies in $A$'s
declared comparison set. If
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{multi}}(c)+E_{R,\mathrm{multi}}(c)<D_{\mathrm{one}}(c),
 \label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
then the feasible recurrent policy has smaller forward KL than the feasible
attention one-call product sampler.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The recurrent KL is the left-hand side. Every one-call product sampler has KL
at least $D_{\mathrm{one}}$, since its estimation error is nonnegative.
\end{proof}
For a declared context law $\rho$, the same argument applies to
$\mathbb E_\rho C_M$ and $\mathbb E_\rho\KL$ by averaging both sides.
Our empirical-panel check uses this mean-cost, mean-KL version; it supplies
neither a per-request deadline nor pointwise quality guarantees.

The comparison is restricted to the declared policies and measured budget. It
does not exclude more efficient untested attention implementations, correlated
output heads, context-dependent or randomized mixtures of the declared
policies, or a causal sampler. In particular, cached causal RWKV also admits
a dependence-free sequential factorization and need not reread the prefix for
each output token.

\paragraph{The missing long-context link.}
Increasing $H$ does not itself change the dependence among $N$ answer tokens.
The learned error $E_{M,\pi}(H,\mathrm{location})$ must be measured as evidence
moves farther from the query. Bounds in generated length $N$ cannot establish
an advantage in clamped context length $H$. Our claim would be supported only
where conditional competence and the measured cost conditions hold together.

\section{A minimal decisive experiment}
\subsection{Paired conditional tasks}
Each public input specifies eight named output coordinates and four independent
binary linear equations. Output slots retain their public coordinate labels.
A base problem is paired across prefix lengths and public-block positions.
The earlier study uses short, 1K, 4K, and 16K inputs; the matched study uses
16K primary inputs and short, 4K, and 32K secondary cells. Irrelevant archive records are
inserted before and after the original instruction/evidence block, placing it
near, midway, or far from the answer. The final answer footer remains next to
the output. Actual token lengths, record counts, and block spans are recorded.
Whole-record packing can undershoot the requested length slightly: the
matched RWKV primary prefixes contain 16,383 tokens, while the attention
cost prefixes contain 16,384 tokens under the same requested 16K budget.
Reported context lengths count prefix tokens; public labels add a fixed
$O(N)$ output scaffold. Moving instructions and equations together does not
isolate factual retention from instruction retention.

The \emph{systematic} control fixes four coordinates and leaves four independent
fair bits, so $D_\pi=0$ for every policy. The \emph{paired-parity} family instead
specifies four disjoint two-coordinate equations. After a public coordinate
permutation, its uniform posterior has the form
\begin{equation}
 Y=(U,U\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}v),\qquad
 U\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(\{0,1\}^4).
\end{equation}
Both families have 16 valid outputs. For paired parity, one-call sampling
loses $4\log2$ nats of dependence. Revealing an information set, then its
complement, has zero within-group dependence error. The information set is
computed from the public equation structure; it does not use a sampled answer.
This deliberately controlled task tests the proposed mechanism rather than
open-ended text generation.

\begin{corollary}[Concrete learned-error threshold]
If the eight conditional marginal KL errors of the two-call sampler average
at most $\epsilon(H)$, then
\begin{equation}
 \KL(P_c\Vert Q_{R,\mathrm{two},c})\le8\epsilon(H),\qquad
 Q_{R,\mathrm{two},c}(\operatorname{supp}P_c)\ge e^{-8\epsilon(H)}.
\end{equation}
If $\epsilon(H)<\log(2)/2$, it has strictly smaller forward KL than every
one-call product sampler on this target law.
\end{corollary}
Validity is reported alongside KL within the valid support: a concentrated
sampler can be valid while failing the uniform target distribution.

\subsection{Interventions and implementation costs}
The reveal-policy intervention holds the learned checkpoint and complete input fixed,
changing only the reveal policy: one call, two information-set calls, or two
calls that each reveal two intact dependent pairs. The two-call policies have
equal group sizes. Let $m_\pi$ count pairs revealed within the same group.
Then $D_\pi=m_\pi\log2$, and exact oracle marginal sampling has valid mass
$2^{-m_\pi}$. The intact-pair policy therefore has $D=4\log2$ and oracle
validity $1/16$, whereas the information-set policy has $D=0$ and oracle
validity one. Learned estimation error is measured for both policies; equal
call count does not guarantee equal conditional competence. The observed
contrast therefore contains both dependence and estimation terms; it is not
an isolated causal measurement of dependence error alone.

Exact enumeration of the 16 target-support endpoints gives joint forward KL,
valid mass, within-valid KL, and the $D/E$ decomposition. Earlier short-task
experiments also retain random partitions and sequential reveals
(Appendix~\ref{app:prior}). In particular, sequential factorization has $D=0$
but can fail when its conditional histories are poorly learned.

The matched 16K cost check compares the recurrent denoiser with a
405M-parameter Pythia bidirectional softmax denoiser on the same public
conditions. Native pretraining and tokenizers differ; at equal native-token
budgets, irrelevant-record counts also differ. These are practical costs,
not an architecture-causal quality ablation. An earlier separate-task screen
also includes causal RWKV with prefix prefill and cached steps
(Appendix~\ref{app:lookup}). Each batch-one request uses one H100; ranks
process independent conditions. Full requests include every
denoising call or the causal prefill, with warmup and CUDA synchronization.
Shared audit caches are never used for timing. Cached and full causal
predictions pass the declared numerical tolerance under IEEE arithmetic.

\subsection{Selected training lineage and preceding evidence}
Only seed 71 in the earlier three-seed campaign learns the paired posterior;
seeds 53 and 89 remain near chance. We select its fixed independent terminal
checkpoint for the controlled mechanism study, retaining all six earlier
outcomes. Both distance-intervention arms start from this same checkpoint.
An earlier fixed 200-update adaptation at mostly 1K inputs improves performance
at its trained length but fails the 16K aggregate. That completed study and all
640 evaluated conditions are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:context-study}.
The new study controls training length at 16K and varies public-block position.

\paragraph{Scope of the evidence.}
The completed earlier three-seed study learns dependence only for seed 71;
all four seed-53 and seed-89 branches remain near chance. The selected-lineage
results here do not establish reliable learning across independent initial
training lineages. The task has
a short binary answer law, public equation structure, and synthetic distractors.
It does not establish broad language-model quality or a learned scheduler.
The context is retained in full, so the result does not show recovery of
information discarded by a state-only memory system. The implementation uses
FLA RWKV kernels; numerical and document-isolation checks pass, while parity
with the official RWKV implementation remains unverified.

The measured boundary is concrete: inexpensive additional scans help when
their conditional predictions remain accurate enough. The earlier 1K adaptation
fails at 16K; the matched 16K intervention reduces distant conditional error and
realizes the predicted reveal-policy advantage. The combination supports the
stated mechanism and budget certificate on this synthetic law. An aggregate
certificate alone does not establish uniformly competent distant evidence use,
as the retained near-only seed 20271013 demonstrates.

\section{Related work}
Masked discrete diffusion supplies the probabilistic construction
\citep{d3pm,mdlm}. Linear Transformers separate a finite-feature kernel from its
recurrent implementation \citep{lineartransformer}; RWKV-7 develops the
generalized delta-rule recurrence used here \citep{rwkv7}. DiffuMamba,
Triplet-Block Diffusion RWKV, and retrofitted linear-attention diffusion already
combine efficient sequence mixers with diffusion
\citep{diffumamba,triplet,retrofit}. We therefore do not claim the first recurrent
diffusion language model. Analyses of factorized masked diffusion and
conditional total correlation already address parallel sampling error
\citep{factorization2025,ctcdepth2026,intrinsicdependence2026}.
Information-theoretic discrete diffusion also connects denoising losses with
mutual information and conditional likelihood \citep{infodiscrete2025}.
Our contribution is a controlled, cost-accounted test of a specific
operating-point criterion, an explicit coverage analysis, and a matched
training-distance intervention with exact endpoint and counterfactual audits.

\section{Exact endpoint accounting}
For a fixed partition the target factorizes by groups, while the model
factorizes within groups:
\[
 P(y\mid c)=\prod_k P(y_{B_k}\mid y_{V_k},c),\qquad
 Q(y\mid c)=\prod_k\prod_{i\in B_k}q^i(y_i\mid y_{V_k},c).
\]
Insert and subtract $\sum_{k,i\in B_k}\log P(y_i\mid y_{V_k},c)$ inside
$\E_P\log(P/Q)$. The two resulting terms are exactly $D_\pi$ and $E_{M,\pi}$.
The averages use target histories, not model rollout histories. Enumerating
valid target support therefore suffices for forward KL; it does not estimate
the probability of invalid paths by omitting them. Their total mass is obtained
by subtracting the exactly summed valid endpoint probability from one.

For valid support $S$ and $V=Q(S)>0$,
\[
 \KL(P\Vert Q)=-\log V+\KL(P\Vert Q(\cdot\mid S)).
\]
Nonnegativity gives $V\ge e^{-\KL(P\Vert Q)}$. For the dependent task the
first four bits are independent and the second group is deterministic given
the first and the public value; hence $D_{\rm two}=0$. Each individual bit is
fair and the answer entropy is $4\log2$, so $D_{\rm one}=8\log2-4\log2$.
This proves the concrete threshold and validity certificate.

For a general partition of the four independent pairs, a pair revealed in
different groups is sampled as one fair bit followed by a deterministic
conditional bit, contributing zero dependence error. Oracle conditionals on
impossible target histories may be extended arbitrarily: once a committed
history violates a completed pair, it cannot lead to a valid endpoint. A pair revealed within
one group is sampled as two fair bits and contributes $\log2$, with probability
$1/2$ of satisfying its equation. Independence across pairs proves
$D_\pi=m_\pi\log2$ and oracle validity $2^{-m_\pi}$. Among the 70 uniformly
selected four/four partitions, the counts for $m_\pi=0,2,4$ are 16,48,6.
Consequently expected dependence is $(12/7)\log2$ and expected oracle validity
is $227/560$. These are analytic predictions; they are not learned-model
measurements or a substitute for the fixed realized partitions in the audit.

\section{History coverage and conditional excess risk}
\label{app:coverage}
A prediction query $z$ includes the public context, visible coordinates and
values, target coordinate, and model stage input. Write $e(z)$ for its
conditional marginal KL. Then $E_{M,\pi}=N\mathbb E_{\mu_\pi}e(z)$.
If $\mu_\pi\ll\nu$ with density ratio at most $\kappa$,
\[
 E_{M,\pi}=N\mathbb E_\nu\!\left[\frac{d\mu_\pi}{d\nu}e(z)\right]
 \le N\kappa\mathbb E_\nu e(z).
\]
The expectation on the right is supervised cross-entropy minus the exact
conditional-entropy floor. Both sides must use the same context law; this is
not a generalization bound from training keys to test keys or longer contexts.
For balanced two-round supervision on the eight-bit task, population excess
risk is $E_{M,\mathrm{info}}/8$. If this objective has mixture weight $\alpha$,
nonnegativity of the other objective's excess risk gives
$E_{M,\mathrm{info}}\le(8/\alpha)\mathcal R_{\mathrm{mixture}}$.
A family-specific guarantee requires its conditional context law, rather than
using the combined-family risk without adjusting for the sampling probability.

For the necessity of coverage, set predictions to the exact oracle on the
empty and first-four-visible histories used by the information-set policy.
On its unvisited one-bit-visible history, give the next, still-fair bit
probability $\varepsilon$ of being one. Leave other predictions at the oracle.
The two-call KL is zero, but sequential KL includes
\[
 \KL(\operatorname{Bern}(1/2)\Vert\operatorname{Bern}(\varepsilon))
 =-\log2-\tfrac12\log[\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)]\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Both policies have $D=0$. Ordinary denoising can supply coverage, but rare
histories may leave a large density ratio. The observed seed-71 policy mismatch
is compatible with this argument; it does not identify its internal causal
mechanism or establish a long-context effect.


\paragraph{Exact mask-coverage constants.}
Generalize the implemented objective to $N$ uniformly sampled stages,
masking probability $p=t/N$, and weight $1/t$ per masked coordinate.
For a particular mask $M$ and $i\in M$, the normalized supervision weight is
$p^{|M|}(1-p)^{N-|M|}/(Nt)$. A policy query has weight $1/N$ and uses stage
$t=|M|$, giving density ratio
\[
 \frac{t}{(t/N)^t(1-t/N)^{N-t}}.
\]
For an even-$N$ balanced two-round policy, the sharp maximum is
$(N/2)2^N$, attained in round two; round one has ratio $N$.
At $N=8$, equal mixing with information-set supervision yields
$\kappa_{\rm info}=2048/1025<2$, but
$\kappa_{\rm sequential}\approx1988.411$ for the canonical first-four
information set (and $2048$ for a last-four information set), while
$\kappa_{\rm pair\text{-}preserving}=2048$.
Enumeration of all masks verifies these constants. The corresponding bound
on total estimation error is $N\kappa\mathcal R_\nu$.
This quantifies a limitation of changing inference policies after training.
It is a worst-case coverage statement about answer length $N$, not an actual
sample-complexity lower bound, an explanation proved for a particular trained
network, or an advantage in context length $H$.

\section{Earlier checkpoint and posterior evidence}
\label{app:prior}
The frozen F2 checkpoint study contains 52,800 accepted synthetic examples per
model across supplied lengths 16K, 32K, and 64K. F2 and released causal RWKV
obtain 15 and 14 exact matches. Conditional macro accuracies are 0.030864\%
and 0.028807\%; their difference is 0.002058 percentage points, with a paired
conditional 95\% bootstrap interval of $[-0.020576,0.024691]$. Sixty unsupported
cells are exclusions. Evidence-only controls yield zero correct and zero
parseable answers out of 72 per model. These outcomes do not isolate retention.

A separate 455M development study compares independent and complementary
policy histories from the same seed-17 parent. At update 2,500, information-set
parity KL is 2.800646 and 2.772621 nats, with valid mass 0.062189 and 0.062500:
both remain near the $1/16$ baseline. The frozen three-seed replication retains
all six terminal branches (Table~\ref{tab:prior-terminal}). Only seed 71 learns
the supervised information-set law. Its independent and complementary branches
have eight-call sequential KL 11.447909 and 14.174383 nats: additional calls
alone do not deliver the learned law. All seed-53 and seed-89 branches remain
near chance. These results expose training-seed and policy-history sensitivity;
a successful seed does not establish reliable learning. The lookup task changes
the input/output interface and is reported separately (Appendix~\ref{app:lookup}).
