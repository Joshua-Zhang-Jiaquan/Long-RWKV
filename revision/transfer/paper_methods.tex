\subsection{Predicting reveal schedules on unseen dependency classes}
The fixed-partition decomposition identifies a prediction target but does not
predict learned error on a new context. We test whether source-class calibration
can supply that missing prediction. Let $U$ be uniform on four binary variables
and $P=BU\oplus s$, where $B$ is invertible over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ and $s$ is public.
Both $U$ and $P$ have independent fair marginals. Revealing either basis first
therefore gives a two-round policy with $D=0$. Both policies read the same full
context twice. Their forward-KL difference is entirely a difference in learned
conditional error, so dependence accounting alone cannot rank them. All eight unconditional
marginals are fair and the joint entropy is $4\log2$, so every one-call product
sampler has forward KL at least $8\log2-4\log2=4\log2$.

We enumerate the 20,160 invertible matrices and partition their 25 homogeneous-code
weight-enumerator classes by a fixed hash: 15 source classes and 10 held-out
classes. Different weight enumerators certify that no permutation of the eight
answer coordinates maps one code to the other. Equal enumerators need not imply
equivalent codes. Training samples only source classes. Prompts randomize labels,
coordinate order, equation order, and the names of the two publicly displayed
bases. The selector obtains basis membership from the prompt rather than from
private generator labels or target values.

Six independent task-training pipelines start from the same public pretrained
backbone, using separate data and conditioning-initialization seeds. Each runs
300 two-bit updates, 400 four-bit updates, 1,800 source-code updates, and 600
16K updates with balanced public-block positions. Every terminal checkpoint is
retained. This tests fresh lineages for the new task; it does not retroactively
replicate the earlier position intervention across independent initial models.

For each model, source calibration comprises four fresh prompts in each source
class at three context positions. We estimate per-coordinate conditional error
using public features: fair versus deterministic marginal, parity fan-in,
number of displayed equations combined (column degree for fair marginals), and
position. A fixed binned estimator shrinks toward a round/position mean. A bin
requires eight distinct source prompts; unsupported decisions fall back to a
predeclared content-hash tie and remain in the primary denominator. First-round
marginal error is included. No monotonic relation between parity fan-in and
neural error is assumed.

Predicted scores and chosen policies for eight fresh prompts per held-out class,
at all three positions, are hashed before any held-out neural evaluation.
Exact evaluation then enumerates all 16 target endpoints under both policies.
The primary estimand is KL reduction against the dependence-only content-hash
tie. We also report fixed-basis, random-in-expectation, source-unconditional,
and uncalibrated fan-in baselines, together with regret to the retrospective
best policy. Uncertainty resamples training lineages and held-out code classes,
then prompts within classes, retaining their paired positions.

The predeclared success criteria require a mean improvement of at least 0.1 nat,
a positive 95\% interval, at least 80\% supported decisions, six complete
lineages, and at least five models whose mean selected-policy KL is below the
one-call product floor $4\log2$. A gain over a dependence-only tie does not by
itself establish that calibration improves on the structural heuristic.
Cross-class error prediction is an empirical transfer hypothesis: the full-context
training/test density ratio is infinite, so the preceding finite-coverage bound
does not certify this extrapolation.
